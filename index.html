The 5 specs that actually decide how a budget laptop feels

SpecDon't go belowWhy
RAM8 GB (16 GB if you can)The #1 cause of a "slow" cheap laptop. 4 GB will choke on a few browser tabs.
Storage256 GB SSDAn SSD matters more than the CPU for everyday snappiness. Avoid eMMC and spinning HDDs.
CPURyzen 5 / Core i5 (or equivalent)Fine for work, browsing, media. You rarely need more on a budget.
Display1080p (FHD), IPSSkip 768p panels — they age the experience faster than any other spec.
BuildMetal hinge / lid if possibleThe first thing to break on cheap laptops is the hinge.
Rule of thumb: a laptop with 16 GB RAM + a 512 GB SSD + a 1080p IPS screen at a budget price will out-feel a "faster CPU" model with 8 GB and a dim 768p panel every single time.

What you're usually overpaying for

  • Discrete graphics you won't use. Unless you game or edit video, integrated graphics are fine — see our notes on picking the right laptop by use-case.
  • Touchscreens. Nice-to-have, rarely worth the premium on a budget build.
  • Brand tax. Comparable specs vary wildly in price — this is where buying from a value-focused store pays off.

Quick Linux-compatibility checklist

Buying a cheap laptop to run Linux? Check these before you commit:

  • Wi-Fi chipset — Intel and MediaTek cards have the best out-of-the-box driver support; some Realtek cards need extra work.
  • Avoid Intel "Evo"-only exotic sleep states if you want reliable suspend/resume on older kernels.
  • Prefer standard SSDs (NVMe/SATA) over soldered eMMC for easy installs.
  • Check the fingerprint reader & webcam model — the two most common "works on Windows, fiddly on Linux" parts.

A well-chosen budget laptop makes a great, cheap Linux machine — the RAM and SSD guidance above matters even more here.
